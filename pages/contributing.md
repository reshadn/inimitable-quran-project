# Contribute to the project

You are welcome to help whether you agree with the argument, disagree with it, or have not decided. The most useful submissions identify a specific claim, explain the issue, and supply evidence another reader can inspect.

Start in [the public repository](https://github.com/reshadn/inimitable-quran-project). Open an issue to discuss a correction or proposal. Use a pull request for a concrete edit. Please begin with a small contribution so its reasoning and sources can receive careful review.

## Where help is needed

- **Arabic and translation:** Correct grammatical analysis, identify plausible alternative readings, or explain what a working translation adds or loses.
- **Sources and editions:** Verify an attribution, edition, page, or quotation against a lawful copy. Distinguish the printed page from a digital viewer's page number.
- **Literary comparison:** Propose strong Arabic alternatives, with provenance and an explanation of what makes the comparison fair.
- **Methods and analysis:** Improve annotations, reproduce a result, expose a confound, or propose a test that could weaken the project's claims.
- **Reader access:** Improve explanations, accessibility, navigation, or a clearly attributed translation of the project's original material.

## Before submitting

Include the chapter, passage, source ID, or file concerned. Quote only what is necessary to locate the issue. Explain your proposed correction and why it matters.

For source-based claims, provide the author, title, edition, exact location, and a lawful link where possible. State what you actually inspected: a catalog record, an abstract, selected pages, or a complete work. Explain any dependence on another source and any remaining uncertainty.

For code or numerical claims, provide inputs, versions, counting or normalization rules, reproduction steps, and the expected result. Distinguish an exploratory pattern from a prospectively specified test. Do not describe a measure of style, frequency, or recall as proof of revelation.

For Arabic alternatives, clearly label them as analytical wording, not Qur’anic text. For translations, identify the translator and distinguish a proposed rendering from an established quotation.

## How review works

1. A contributor opens an issue describing the claim and evidence.
2. Maintainers check the source, reasoning, reproduction steps, and relevant rights. Additional language or methods review may be needed.
3. Maintainers record a decision: accept, request revision, defer, or decline, with reasons.
4. Accepted changes enter the published project and its changelog.

A submitted change is a proposal until accepted. Public issues and pull requests may be visible before a decision; that visibility is not project endorsement. Community review must not be described as completed independent academic validation. Material disagreements and unresolved limitations should remain visible in the research record.

## Conduct and permissions

Challenge claims, including the project's central claim. Treat people respectfully. Harassment, personal attacks, and hostility toward religious or nonreligious communities are not acceptable contributions.

Submit only material you have permission to share. Link to copyrighted works instead of uploading unauthorized copies. Do not post private personal data, participant identities, confidential correspondence, or recordings without the necessary rights and consent.

The licenses are CC BY 4.0 for original prose and MIT for code; check the repository's current license files before contributing. Identify third-party material and its terms separately. Do not relicense it by including it in a submission.
