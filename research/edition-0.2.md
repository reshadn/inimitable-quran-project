# Expanded research edition 0.2

Published 20 September 2026 as a public working draft. The manuscript now contains an opening essay and six chapters, approximately 14,600 whitespace-delimited words including notes and markup. This is an editorial size, not an Arabic linguistic measurement.

Four chapters are new: **The Challenge**, **The Distance Between Claims**, **A History of the Question**, and **Counting Without Overclaiming**. They develop the challenge passages, the argument's inferential steps, selected classical debates, and a practical measurement program. The two earlier close readings remain in the book. Chapter numbers follow the proposed full book map; intervening planned chapters have not been written yet.

The bibliography now contains 71 records: 16 with bounded passage or documentation inspection, 29 with catalog or abstract confirmation, and 26 discovery leads. Al-Rummānī and al-Khaṭṭābī have advanced from discovery leads to scoped inspection of digital Arabic transcriptions. Six additional records cover surah structure, explanatory reasoning, and statistical methods. No complete work is claimed as read. No source's inclusion implies endorsement of the project.

The four new chapter ledgers contain 26 source records. These overlap with the bibliography, earlier chapter records, and one another. They are not 26 independent arguments or 26 additional unique works. The combined chapter-evidence view provides all 38 scoped records for inspection.

The corpus calculation is unchanged: the preserved Tanzil Uthmani 1.1 file, its hash, and the declared counting rules remain the baseline. The new measurement chapter's 40-of-50 illustration is invented teaching arithmetic, explicitly labeled as such. No expert panel, reader experiment, recitation study, comparative superiority finding, or numerical probability of revelation has been produced.

Drafting, source checks, and editorial cross-checks were AI-assisted. They do not count as independent human scholarly review. Digital transcriptions, page-image checks, abstracts, and catalog records are identified separately in the ledgers. The novel applications are proposals whose novelty and performance have not been established.

## What would improve this edition

Priority contributions are independent Arabic and philosophical review, confirmation of printed editions, stronger defenses of comparison texts, and fuller reading of modern scholarship. Proposed studies need protocols, instruments, permissions, and appropriate human review before recruitment or data collection. Contributions enter through the public review process; a submission is not an accepted finding.
